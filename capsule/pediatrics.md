### seizures

**Clinical stem / question**

> classification of the seizure by (ILAE - International League Against Epilepsy).

**Core concept / trap**

> 1.Focal / Partial Seizures
>
>   a. Focal Aware Seizures :- Carbamazepine 
>   b. Focal Impaired Awareness Seizures :-  Carbamazepine
>   c. Focal to Bilateral Tonic-Clonic :- Carbamazepine or Levetiracetam / Lamotrigine
>
> 2.Generalized Seizures
> A. Motor Seizure:- 
>  a. Tonic-Clonic Seizures (Grand Mal Seizures):- Sodium Valproate or Alternatives: Levetiracetam, Lamotrigine
>  b. Tonic Seizures :- Sodium Valproate
>  c.  Clonic Seizures :-  Sodium Valproate
>  d. Myoclonic Seizures :- : Sodium Valproate  (Note: Avoid Carbamazepine and Phenytoin as they can exacerbate myoclonic jerks)
>  e. Atonic Seizures (Drop Attacks) :- Sodium Valproate (Alternatives: Lamotrigine, Clobazam, Rufinamide)
>
> B. Non-Motor / Absence Seizures:- 
>  a. Absence Seizures (Petit Mal Seizures):- 
> Ethosuximide (First-line for pure/uncomplicated absence seizures)
> Sodium Valproate (First-line if absence seizures coexist with generalized tonic-clonic seizures)
>
> 3. Unknown onset seizure:-Broad-Spectrum Anti-Epileptic Drugs (AEDs): Sodium Valproate, Levetiracetam, or Lamotrigine
> note narrow spectrum agent like Carbamazepine can worsen unsuspected generalized or absence/myoclonic seizures.
>
> 4. special pediatrics syndrome 
> a. febrile seizures:-Acute / Prolonged Episode: Rectal (PR) Diazepam (or Intranasal/IV Midazolam).
> Prophylaxis/Symptomatic: Antipyretics (Acetaminophen/Ibuprofen) + cooling measures
> b. West's syndrome(infantile spasm) Syndrome (Infantile Spasms):ACTH (Adrenocorticotropic Hormone)or High-Dose Oral Corticosteroids.
> Vigabatrin is the specific Drug of Choice if West's syndrome is associated with Tuberous Sclerosis Complex.
> c. lennox- Gastaut Syndrome:-First-Line / Broad-Spectrum: Sodium Valproate combined with Lamotrigine.
> FDA-Approved Adjunctive Agents: Rufinamide, Topiramate, Clobazam, and Cannabidiol (CBD)

- **Confidence:** Med
- **Frequency:** Not sure

### Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection \(TAPVC\)

**Clinical stem / question**

> Supracardiac TAPVC 	
> Snowman Sign / Figure-of-8 on xray. and Infracardiac TAPVC  Severe Pulmonary Edema / White-out lungs on xray and medical emergency after the birth tachypneic cynoisis and the po2 is not increase by the 100% of the oxygen.

**Core concept / trap**

> TAPVC occurs when all four pulmonary veins fail to connect directly to the left atrium and instead drain anomalously into the right heart circulation, requiring an interatrial right-to-left shunt (ASD or PFO) to maintain systemic cardiac output.
> A. Supracardiac TAPVC (Draining to Left Innominate Vein) 
> Anatomical Course: The pulmonary veins coalesce behind the heart into a common sinus, which ascends via a vertical vein to drain into the left innominate (brachiocephalic) vein into Superior Vena Cava (SVC) into the  Right Atrium. Usually NON-OBSTRUCTED Presents over weeks to months as high pulmonary blood flow leads to progressive heart failure, mild cyanosis, and cardiomegaly (classic "Snowman sign" or "Figure-of-8" appearance on chest X-ray in older infants).
> B. Infracardiac TAPVC (Draining to Portal Venous System)
> Anatomical Course: The common pulmonary venous sinus drains into a long descending vertical vein that passes through the diaphragm (via the esophageal hiatus) to join the portal vein, ductus venosus, or hepatic veins to  Inferior Vena Cava (IVC) to Right Atrium.
> Obstruction Risk: Almost ALWAYS OBSTRUCTED. Obstruction occurs due to:
> Mechanical compression of the descending vein at the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm.
> High vascular resistance of the hepatic capillary sinusoids.
> Constriction of the ductus venosus after birth. 
> Clinical Timing: Presents within the first hours to days of life as a critical neonatal emergency with severe cyanosis, pulmonary congestion, and cardiogenic/obstructive shock.
> Hyperoxia Test Result: Refractory cyanosis despite 100%O2 confirms a fixed Right-to-Left intracardiac shunt (cyanotic congenital heart disease)
>  Obstructed TAPVC (Infracardiac) Suspected
>  ├── 1. Emergency Bedside Echocardiogram (Confirm Diagnosis &amp; Rule out Obstruction)
>  ├── 2. Endotracheal Intubation &amp; Mechanical Ventilation (High PEEP for Pulmonary Edema)
>  ├── 3. Inotropic Support &amp; Acidosis Correction
>  └── 4. IMMEDIATE EMERGENCY SURGICAL REPAIR (Definitive Cure)
> A. Definitive Treatment: Emergency Surgical Repair
> Emergency Surgery is Mandatory: Obstructed TAPVC is a surgical emergency. Medical management alone cannot relieve the anatomical venous obstruction.
> Surgical Steps:
> Anastomosis of the common pulmonary venous sinus to the posterior wall of the Left Atrium.
> Ligation of the anomalous vertical vein.
> Closure of the Atrial Septal Defect (ASD)
