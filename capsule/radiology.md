### Nuclear Medicine

**Clinical stem / question**

> Nuclear medicine imaging is a specialized diagnostic branch that uses radiopharmaceuticals (radioisotopes conjugated with biological molecules) to evaluate physiological, metabolic, and tissue-level functional activity rather than purely anatomical structure

**Core concept / trap**

> 1.Positron Emission Tomography (PET / PET-CT):- ¹⁸F-Fluorodeoxyglucose (¹⁸F-FDG) is the most common tracer, measuring tissue glucose metabolism. Based on the detection of paired annihilation photons (511 keV) moving in opposite directions (180°)
> indication:- Myocardial Viability: PET is the gold standard for assessing hibernating or ischemic myocardium
> Oncology: Staging, restaging, assessing response to therapy, and detecting recurrence in malignancies (e.g., lymphoma, lung cancer, breast carcinoma, colorectal cancer) it is never used in the diagnosis of the carcinoma.
> Neurology: Functional localization of epileptogenic foci.
>
> 2.Single-Photon Emission Computed Tomography (SPECT / SPECT-CT):-Uses 3D tomographic reconstruction from rotating gamma camera acquisitions.
> Clinical Indications:
> Parathyroid Adenoma Localization: Sestamibi SPECT / SPECT-CT is the investigation of choice for 3D pre-operative localization of hyperfunctioning parathyroid adenomas or ectopic glands.
> Myocardial Perfusion: Evaluates regional myocardial blood flow using ²⁰¹Tl or ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MIBI.
>
> 3.Planar Scintigraphy (Gamma Camera Imaging)
> Two-dimensional functional imaging used for routine bone scans, renal dynamic scans, and thyroid uptake studies
> radio phanmaceuticals:- 
> A. Technetium-99m (⁹⁹ᵐTc) Derivatives
> Physical Properties: Half-life = 6 hours ;derived from Molybdenum-99 (⁹⁹Mo) generators
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Sestamibi (MIBI): Investigation of choice for pre-operative localization of single or ectopic parathyroid adenomas due to delayed radiotracer retention compared to rapid washout from thyroid tissue , Also used in myocardial perfusion imaging.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-HIDA / Mebrofenin (Cholescintigraphy): Functional gold standard for confirming active post-cholecystectomy bile leaks (extravasation into peritoneal cavity)
> , acute cholecystitis (cystic duct obstruction), and differentiating biliary atresia in infants.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Polyphosphate / MDP Bone Scan: Detects osteoblastic response in skeletal metastases (e.g., prostate cancer), fractures, and osteomyelitis. Note: Bone scans cannot measure bone mineral density and are NOT used to diagnose osteoporosis (DEXA is used instead).
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA: Measures Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) as it is excreted solely by glomerular filtration.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAG3: Measures Effective Renal Plasma Flow (ERPF), vesicoureteric reflux, and renal transplant rejection.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DMSA: Preferred agent for evaluating renal cortical scarring.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Tagged Red Blood Cells (RBCs): Used for locating gastrointestinal bleeding (detects slow bleeding at rates of 0.1–0.4 mL/min), confirming cavernous hemangiomas, and radionuclide ventriculography.
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Sulfur Colloid: Taken up by hepatic Kupffer cells; used for liver/spleen lymphoscintigraphy
> ⁹⁹ᵐTc-Pyrophosphate: Used for avid myocardial infarction imaging ("hot spot" in acute MI).
> B. IODINE ISOTOPES 
> Iodine-131 (¹³¹I): Half-life = 8 days. Used for post-surgical whole-body scans and ablation therapy in differentiated thyroid carcinoma.
> Iodine-123 (¹²³I): Half-life = 13 hours. Diagnostic thyroid uptake and imaging.
> ¹²³I / ¹³¹I-MIBG (Metaiodobenzylguanidine): Concentrates in neuroendocrine cells via norepinephrine transporters; choice scanner for pheochromocytoma (extra-adrenal/metastatic) and neuroblastoma
> .
> ¹³¹I-Orthohippurate (Hippuran): Assesses effective renal plasma flow.
> C. Other Important Radiotracers:- 
> Thallium-201 (²⁰¹Tl): Potassium analog used in myocardial ischemia/viability studies ("cold spots" in infarcted tissue)
>  and brain tumor/SOL imaging.
> Gallium-67 (⁶⁷Ga): Half-life = 78 hours. Concentrates in inflammatory exudates, abscesses, and residual lymphoma masses. Produces the Panda sign in sarcoidosis.
> Indium-111 Pentetreotide (Somatostatin Receptor Scintigraphy / SRS): Binds to somatostatin receptors on neuroendocrine tumors (carcinoid, insulinoma).
> ¹⁸F-Fluoromisonidazole: Specific tracer for imaging tumor hypoxia.

- **Confidence:** Med
- **Frequency:** Not sure

### nuclear medicine

**Clinical stem / question**

> Diagnostic Summary by Organ System

**Core concept / trap**

> Cardiovascular  
> Myocardial Viability (¹⁸F-FDG PET) Gold standard for hibernating myocardium
> Myocardial Ischemia / Perfusion(²⁰¹Tl / ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MIBI SPECT)  Detects reversible ischemia vs. permanent scar
>
> Endocrine 
> Parathyroid Adenoma(⁹⁹ᵐTc-Sestamibi SPECT) Persistent retention in adenoma on delayed phase
> Pheochromocytoma / Neuroblastoma(¹²³I / ¹³¹I-MIBG Scan) High sensitivity for extra-adrenal &amp; metastatic disease
>
> Hepatobiliary 
> Active Bile Leak(⁹⁹ᵐTc-HIDA Scan) Functional demonstration of bile extravasation
> Gastrointestinal 
> Lower GI Bleeding(⁹⁹ᵐTc-Labeled RBC Scan) Detects slow active bleeding (0.1–0.4 mL/min).
>
> Nephrology 
> GFR Measurement(⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA Scan) Excreted purely via glomerular filtration
> Renal Plasma Flow (ERPF) (⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAG3 / ¹³¹I-Hippuran) Evaluates tubular secretion and obstructive uropathy.
>
> Musculoskeletal Bone Metastases (⁹⁹ᵐTc-Polyphosphate Bone Scan) High sensitivity for osteoblastic activity
>
>
>  Radiation Dosimetry Overview
> SPECT Scan Dose: Typically 2–5 mSv.
> PET-CT Scan Dose: Typically 5–15 mSv.
